# CREDITS

Third-party work this skill substantially builds upon, per Standard §15.3.

This skill is a distribution in its own right — installed from a bundle without
the consumer ever seeing the source repository — which is the same reasoning
§5.6 uses to require the license text in every bundle. The credit travels with
it for the same reason. The published Standard carries the matching record at
`standard/CREDITS.md`.

## ISO/IEC/IEEE 29148:2018

*Systems and software engineering — Life cycle processes — Requirements
engineering.*

Informs **§20 — Requirements Engineering** and **§21 — Verification,
Validation & Traceability**, added at v2.00. Taken as principles: the
separation of stakeholder needs from a requirements specification; the nine
individual and five set characteristics of §20.4; the attribute set each
requirement carries, including the verification method chosen at authoring
time; and the four admissible verification methods of §21.1.

**No text is reproduced.** ISO/IEC/IEEE 29148:2018 is copyrighted and sold
under restrictive terms. Every clause in §20 and §21 is in the Standard's own
words, and the characteristic lists are restatements rather than quotations.
Where 29148 assumes a customer/supplier contract and a tree of deliverable
documents, the Standard substitutes what a single maintainer at hobby pace can
execute (§19).

Copyright ISO/IEC/IEEE. <https://www.iso.org/standard/72089.html>

## ECSS-E-ST-40C

*Space engineering: Software.* European Cooperation for Space Standardization.

Informs **§19 — Assurance Categories, Tailoring & Conformance**, **§21.4**,
**§22 — Resource Budgets & Margins**, **§25 — Baselines, Anomalies & Release
Acceptance**, and **§26 — Operations, Maintenance & Support**, added at v2.00.
Taken as principles: the criticality-category model and its graded obligations;
the recorded tailoring register replacing an ad-hoc "not applicable"; the
staged review sequence, compressed to the three gates of §19.4; the
resource-budget and margin-band discipline of §22; the consequence-based
anomaly classification of §25; and the principle that verification independence
scales with criticality (§21.4).

**No text is reproduced.** ECSS standards are copyrighted by the European
Cooperation for Space Standardization. Where ECSS assumes an independent
verification organization, the Standard obtains independence from a different
*mechanism* rather than a different person (§21.4) — the principles are
imported, the organizational structure is not.

Copyright ECSS / ESA. <https://ecss.nl/>
